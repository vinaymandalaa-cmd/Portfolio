CHAPTER 01 / 2018 → 2022 B.DES INTERIOR DESIGN · JNAFAU

The medium changed.
The instinct didn't.

Four years of interiors taught me proportion, hierarchy, materiality, and how to talk to a client standing in an unfinished room. It also taught me constraints, a wall costs money to move.

Screens had my attention long before rooms did. Somewhere between a site visit and a floor plan I realised the thing I loved about a room was the same thing I'd love about an interface: deciding where a person's attention goes next.

A room is finished when you leave it. An interface keeps being used. Six months: three of fundamentals, UX laws and heuristic evaluation, three of building case studies under review. Nerdy Otaku and Drop. That's where the six-step spine I still fall back on came from.

CHAPTER 03 / 2023 · CHENNAI

FULL Creative

Brand designer + UI/UX intern, three months. I walked in expecting to design screens and instead learned how design actually gets shipped: through other people.

The tasks were small, image swaps, type fixes, marketing assets, small UI updates. The lesson wasn't. Content has to fit the interface. Decisions have to survive review. Developers need assets that work. Auto Layout, components and consistency stopped being vocabulary and became habits, fast.

CHAPTER 05 / MAR 2024 · INCEPTEO

Hired as one designer. Used as four.

Junior UX Designer on the offer letter. In practice I was the only person doing product and marketing. Two designers sat purely on Stratpilot; I backed them up when deadlines closed in and owned everything else. Four months. It felt like a year, in the useful way.

{{ h.n }} {{ h.name }} {{ h.note }}
The turn

Then the CEO started taking me into client rooms.

Requirement gathering, discovery calls, the awkward question nobody wants to ask about scope. On a community platform for autistic users, a UK-based designer ran the meeting and I watched how it's actually done: ask, don't present. Confirm, don't assume. Write the constraint down while they're still in the room.

That's the month I stopped being only a designer and became a designer who understands stakeholders.

I was getting very good at producing screens. I still didn't understand the product behind them.

Why I left Not the company. The gap between a pretty screen and a real product. Two offers on the table. I took the one with a game in it.

CHAPTER 08 / NOW

AI didn't replace the designer. It joined the toolkit.

When the new siya brand had to reach a product that had grown past a hundred screens, a manual redesign was a three-month job. So AI entered the workflow. Claude, Stitch, UX Pilot. The first outputs weren't good enough, and the reason was instructive: AI is only useful inside a defined design system.
